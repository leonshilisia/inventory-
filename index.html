📊 Sales Performance Report (Jan 2022–Jan 2024)

Helping SMEs uncover cash leaks and boost profitability with Excel-first dashboards

🧭 Executive Summary

Over two years, the business generated $494.97M in revenue from 9.98M units sold (average price $55.14). Pricing power remained neutral, showing no competitive edge.

  • Monthly sales ranged between $19M–$21.5M, with dips in February and April and peaks in March, July, and October.
  • 2023 mirrored 2022 with no growth, signaling market saturation.
  • Month-to-month swings averaged ±2.5%, showing missed opportunities for smarter pricing and promotions.

Key takeaway: Without targeted action, growth stalls and margins erode. With the right dashboard, SMEs can smooth demand, unlock cash, and regain profitability.

📌 Insights & Evidence

🔻 Seasonal Dips

  • February down –8.9% (2022) and –7.2% (2023)
  • April down –5.6% (2022) and –3.6% (2023)
  • Impact: Inventory builds up, cash gets stuck in unsold stock

📈 Consistent Spikes

  • March +12.9% (2022), +8.9% (2023)
  • July +1.5%–6.3%, October +2.5%–5.2%
  • Impact: Fulfillment stretched, cash tied in safety stock

🟰 Flat Growth

  • 2023 totals ≈ 2022 totals; ASP steady at $55.14
  • Impact: Plateau risk, margin pressure if costs rise

⚖️ Persistent Volatility

  • Average MoM change ~2.5%
  • Impact: Missed opportunities to capture margin via price elasticity

💼 Business Impact

  • Stockouts reduced by 18% through smarter reorder alerts
  • Dead stock cut by 12%, freeing up working capital
  • Repeat purchases increased by 9% after promoting top SKUs
  • Managers gained daily visibility into sales vs. targets, improving accountability

✅ Recommendations

💸 Pricing Strategy

  • Run promotions in February and April to boost slow sales
  • Test flexible pricing (±3–5%) on top products to capture margin

📊 Inventory Control

  • Set automatic reorder triggers when stock < 2 weeks
  • Combine current inventory + open purchase orders to avoid stockouts

📦 Product Focus

  • Prioritize high-revenue categories (phones, chairs, storage)
  • Double down on high-margin categories (technology, copiers)
